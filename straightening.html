Representation · Geometry

Straightening & predictive coding

Hypothesis: the visual system reshapes the geometry of its representations so the near future becomes easy to predict.

Related publications

The idea

A moving scene traces a curved, tangled path through the space of raw pixels — hard to extrapolate even one frame ahead. A guiding idea from my PhD is that the visual system remedies this by reshaping the geometry of its representations: it maps temporally evolving inputs onto straighter trajectories, where the next moment is close to a linear continuation of the last. Straightening turns prediction from an intractable problem into a nearly geometric one, and connects naturally to predictive-coding accounts of cortex. Evidence consistent with this hypothesis comes from human psychophysics [1], macaque V1 population activity [2], and artificial neural networks [3]. We are continuing this work to see if temporal straightening increases along the visual hierarchy, in collaboration with Robbe Goris, Tien Nguyen, Corey Ziemba, and Olivier Hénaff.

Figure 1. Straightening of temporal trajectories in the representation domain. Incoming visual inputs can be described as a vector in a high-dimensional space that traces out a temporal trajectory. The dimensions of the input space can be thought of as activation levels of individual sensors (photoreceptors). Naturally occurring light patterns typically evolve in a complex, non-linear manner, which poses a challenge for predicting future states (curved blue trajectory). The temporal straightening hypothesis proposes that the visual system transforms its input such that it follows straighter temporal trajectories (straighter red trajectory), thereby facilitating perceptual tasks for future [t+1] predictions from past [t-1, t] representations, as shown in the dotted red arrow.

Where the idea travels

This framework has since been applied well beyond neuroscience. AI-Generated Video Detection via Perceptual Straightening [4] uses representation straightening to tell real video from synthetic: natural clips trace straighter trajectories in a self-supervised model's feature space than generated ones do, and that geometric signature alone drives state-of-the-art detection. It's a clean example of a neuroscience principle becoming a practical measurement.
