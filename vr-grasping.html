3D vision in action

Recovering the brain's 3D perception from VR grasping

Ask me about it

Project led by Weiwen Tian (PhD candidate). My role: co-advisor with Jim DiCarlo in MIT

The question

Grasping is one of the most natural ways we engage the physical world, and it leans on several senses at once — seeing visible parts of an object, along with the hand, proprioception, and touch. Before the hand ever makes contact, though, the brain has already committed to a plan built almost entirely from vision: an internal estimate of the object's 3D shape, including the parts the eyes can't see. This project asks what that visual estimate looks like, using grasp itself as the readout of 3D shapes in virtual reality.

Grasping is a uniquely rich probe because a hand closing around an object reports on both visible and occluded surfaces — something classic psychophysics struggles with screen-based surveys. Forced-choice tasks (match-to-sample, discrimination, depth comparison) can't cover continuous surface and depth variables; continuous-response tasks (gauge-figure surface-normal or depth-profile adjustment) can't collect responses from hidden regions, as well as long-horizon tasks in 3D space. Immersive VR environments touches all of it. To our knowledge this may be the first attempt to visualize the spatial layout of an entire object as the brain represents it, read out purely from natural behavior.

Why virtual reality

VR provides a platform to control sensorimotor behavior (and generate training data for physical AI)

  • Isolated
    Vision as the sole external cue. Tactile feedback is disabled, for now, so the grasp reflects how far we can plan actions with limited viewpoints. Multimodal is a natural segue.
  • Control
    Consistent, repeatable stimuli. The same scene can be shown identically across trials and subjects (both humans and models).
  • Natural
    Immersive and interactive. Unlike screen tasks, the subject grasps in depth with a real reaching movement and interactive feedback.
  • Rich
    Many conditions on tap. Scene variation, hand visibility, and conditions hard to test in real life, such as no gravity, are all software-controlled; the headset renders the world while body-tracking (gaze, hand, and more) records behavior.

Deliverables

  • 1 Tool
    Sensorimotor testbed. An immersive testbed that allows different actuators (hands, robots) to interact in 3D environments, with groundtruth meta data of the environment(with millimeter precision in 3D space)
  • 2 Data
    Human inference data. Measurements associated to shape inference, physical interactions and planning, over time. Accelerates data collection for behavioral data and high-resolution electrophysiology (think BCI).
  • 3 Eval
    Model evaluations and alignment. Enables benchmarks and standardized evaluation of artificial systems to humans. Benchmarks points to where models fall short of human capabilities (e.g., models falling short of identifying camouflaged objects).

Where it's going

We're preparing a paper with new findings around 3D shape inference, as in, the inference quality of 3D structure from limited viewpoints. A quick preview: we first validated our VR platform's resolution, which is sensitive enough to detect differences in human shape inference capabilities across different viewing conditions, and that hand-based measurements are reliable enough to anchor human–model comparisons. So far, with novel abstract shapes, it looks like human shape inferences outperform 3D reconstruction models (across both single- and multi-view inputs). Stay tuned!
